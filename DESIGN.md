# Experience design

Tidepool is a continuous place, viewed through a movable camera. Scrolling changes depth in that place; it never changes document position. One renderer and one scene contain the reef, drifting gardens, kelp cathedral, and midnight archive from the start.

The environment has layered terrain, underwater shafts, marine snow, fish, jellyfish, vegetation, a wreck, and ruins. Ambient motion yields to reduced-motion preferences. Camera movement is damped for wheel input and follows direct touch drags without additional delay.

The interface stays around the edge of the world: identity and collection at the top, depth/location navigation at the edge, and depth controls below. Habitat captions are noninteractive overlays; they cannot intercept touches. The journal is a modal with focus containment, Escape dismissal, and a paused world behind it.

Discoveries are authored objects, not random reward names. Each has a stable ID, model, position, silhouette, hint, and story. Shell and kelp containers open before their objects can be taken. Collecting shrinks and lifts the physical object, announces the find, and adds it to the journal. The final fragment requires all five earlier finds and reveals a starwhale. Collected objects remain absent on later visits to the same browser.

Navigation works by wheel, touch swipe, keyboard, depth slider, and explicit controls. Keyboard users can focus nearby discovery actions that become visible on focus. Browser pinch zoom remains available. Persistent storage and audio failures do not block the expedition.
