# Experience design

Tidepool is a continuous place, viewed through a perspective camera that can orbit through 360 degrees. Scrolling changes depth in that place; it never changes document position. One renderer and one scene contain the reef, drifting gardens, kelp cathedral, and midnight archive from the start.

The environment has one continuous sandy ocean floor beneath the deepest camera stop. Eroded limestone clusters, small instanced stones, ribbon kelp with curled blades and air bladders, seagrass meadows, and branching sea fans are rooted there. Rocks have irregular cut faces, layered fissures, mineral variation, and buried feet. Sand has shallow dunes, fine ripples, and soft contact shadows. There are no repeated rock shelves or floating rock pedestals in the water column. Spatial sun shafts, marine snow, 216 schooling fish, manta rays, jellyfish, a wreck, and ruins fill the descent. Near and far layers move with genuine perspective when the camera turns. Ambient motion yields to reduced-motion preferences. Camera movement is damped for wheel input and follows direct touch drags without additional delay.

The interface stays around the edge of the world: identity and collection at the top, depth/location navigation at the edge, and depth controls below. Habitat captions are noninteractive overlays; they cannot intercept touches. The journal is a modal with focus containment, Escape dismissal, and a paused world behind it.

Discoveries are authored objects, not random reward names. Each has a stable ID, depth, model, silhouette, hint, and story. A saved random seed chooses reachable X/Z positions inside the camera orbit. Its container, wreck, or arch moves with it. Reloading preserves that arrangement. The scallop has a rear hinge and an iridescent bowl; it opens before its pearl can be taken. Drifting kelp and a chest also open. Holding the golden carrier fish for 650 ms triggers a short retch and a visible treasure ejection. Movement or a second finger cancels the hold. Three taps fracture the arch; its existing stones become fading debris. Collecting shrinks and lifts the physical object, announces the find, and adds it to the journal. The final fragment requires all eight other finds and reveals a starwhale, a gathering school, a burst of colored light, and a completion card. Starting a new expedition clears collected IDs, creates a new layout seed, resets containers and interaction state, and returns to the surface using the existing scene and buffers. Collected objects remain absent on later visits to the same browser.

Navigation works by wheel, touch swipe, keyboard, depth slider, and explicit controls. Keyboard users can focus nearby discovery actions that become visible on focus. Horizontal drags, left/right keys, or Shift+wheel orbit the camera without changing depth. Pinching changes the camera zoom; two fingers pan the world. A quick swipe carries decaying momentum. Adding or removing a finger rebases the gesture, and cancellation never collects an object. Holding a discovery reads its clue. Double-tapping open water, the centre-view control, or the 0 key resets the framing. Browser zoom remains available through browser controls and keyboard shortcuts. Persistent storage and audio failures do not block the expedition.

Artist-made swimming fish, manta rays, a sailing wreck, and the final whale establish the visual silhouettes. Their rigs are normalized before placement, material groups are consolidated at export, and only visible creatures animate. Soft environment lighting gives the objects depth without expensive real-time shadows.

Music is an original eight-bar progression at 76 BPM with warm pads, bass, a repeating bell motif, and light percussion. Depth softens the instruments and lengthens the melody tails; the completed collection introduces a second melodic layer. Sound starts only from the user's sound control and includes an adjustable volume.

Hovering and touching create a local water disturbance. Fish steer away and scatter more strongly on a click; dragging moves individual creatures. Leaves bend around the pointer, stone surfaces brighten locally, and clicks rattle rocks and release sediment. The ship and ruins respond with a small movement and bubbles. Interaction particles use a fixed pool, and school geometry is shared across instances.

The moon sits at the shared centre of eight rotating rune bands, one per prerequisite. Original curved calligraphy glows on their surfaces, and each band fragments and fades when its matching discovery is collected. A shared instanced mesh, one small generated atlas, fixed particle arrays, and reusable arch pieces keep these effects bounded. Reset restores their state without creating GPU objects. The drawing buffer is capped by pixel area and reported device memory, and disposal includes shader textures and skeleton resources.
